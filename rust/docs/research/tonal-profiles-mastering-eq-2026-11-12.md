# SlimeyBeats-EZ-Master: tonal profiles, mastering and EQ recommendations

Research date: November 12, 2026. Scope: focused primary-source research and recommendations for the native Rust application. This report changes no code, profiles, skills or settings.

**Recommendation in one paragraph.** Keep Analyze → Master/Re-master in memory → compare/edit → Export. Keep the separate suggested-EQ proposal and shared analyzer/EQ graph. Before handoff, remove any implication that streaming requires one mastering loudness, make the heuristic and per-band nature of suggestions explicit, and distinguish estimated compensation from final export measurements. The main agent has since verified the spectrum bandwidth contract; preserve that behavior and add focused regression coverage. These recommendations do not require another DSP redesign or speculative retuning.

**Evidence and limits.** Inspected only the requested source areas: `src/profiles.rs`, `src/dsp/eq_suggestion.rs`, `src/dsp/eq_loudness.rs`, `src/gui/help.rs`, `src/gui/eq_panel.rs`, `src/config.rs`, and `README.md`. Confidence is **high** for the cited measurement/platform distinctions and inspected constants; **medium** for proposed UX defaults, which need usability and listening validation.

**Seven highest-value conclusions**

1. **Choose mastering intent separately from playback normalization.** ITU-R BS.1770 specifies measurement algorithms; EBU R 128 specifies a broadcast workflow centered on −23 LUFS. Neither prescribes a universal music-streaming master. Spotify currently publishes −14 LUFS for Normal playback, −11 for Loud and −19 for Quiet, with device exceptions. Its delivery guidance recommends −14 LUFS, peaks below −1 dBTP, and below −2 dBTP for masters louder than −14 LUFS. That is Spotify-specific guidance. Apple treats master loudness as a technical and creative choice and says to capture the intended sound regardless of playback volume. Keep a user-selected target and show the achieved loudness when protection prevents reaching it. Do not substitute a universal −16 target either. [S1, Annexes 1–2; S2, recommendation items g–m; S3, “How we adjust loudness” and “Mastering tips”; S4, “Loudness, Dynamic Range, and Clipping” and “Loudness Normalization,” printed pp. 4–5]

2. **An album is an artistic relationship between tracks, not just a processing queue.** Spotify says album playback uses a common gain change, while shuffle and mixed-track contexts normalize individual tracks. Apple also emphasizes intentional contrast within an album. Independently forcing every queued file to the same LUFS can change that relationship. A queue should remain a batch of tracks unless an explicit album workflow exists. A future album mode should preserve intended relative levels, expose the common gain/ceiling constraint, and identify its policy rather than promise identical behavior on every service. [S3, “How we adjust loudness”; S4, printed p. 5]

3. **Genre profiles are tonal/dynamics starting points; reference matching is a different operation.** iZotope's documented Tonal Balance Control design uses ranges derived from numerous tracks and allows single-file or folder targets. FabFilter's EQ Match compares time-averaged input/reference spectra and makes matching detail adjustable through its proposed bands. These are documented methods for tonal guidance and approximation; the inference that they establish an objectively correct genre curve or improved sound is unsupported. The supplied 16 legacy profiles remain heuristics; this research did not validate their numerical targets against a dataset. Do not describe a profile distance as a quality score or compliance result. [S5, “Overview,” “Target Curves,” “Broad View,” “Fine View”; S6, “Step 1: Choose your reference” and “Step 2: Match”]

4. **An EQ response, a spectrum trace and a target spectrum have different meanings.** EQ gain is the filter transfer response. Analyzer tilt is a display adjustment: FabFilter's documented default is 4.5 dB/octave and is configurable. REW explains that FFT spectrum bins have constant bandwidth, whereas fractional-octave RTA bands grow with frequency; consequently pink noise slopes in one representation and is flat in the other. A sloping spectrum therefore does not itself call for corrective EQ. Keep both layers in one graph, with separate named vertical scales, a visible averaging/window mode, and documented spectrum units. Never apply display tilt to DSP targets or to the EQ response. [S7, analyzer-panel options, Tilt/Resolution/Speed; S8, “Spectrum/RTA controls,” Mode]

5. **Deterministic, bounded suggestions are reproducible assistance, not demonstrated improvement.** The inspected algorithm uses 0.25× profile residual plus 0.15× local shape, a 0.5 dB dead zone, 0.25 dB quantization, ±2.5 dB per-band limits and +1 dB boost limits for its bass/air bands. These are product choices, not mastering standards. The octave-neighbor local term cannot establish that a feature is an unwanted resonance rather than instrumentation. Also, overlapping filters combine: a per-band cap does not cap the complete EQ response or output peaks. Preserve the separate proposal and explicit Apply action. Explain small changes in ordinary language; keep coefficients in advanced details. [Local evidence: `eq_suggestion.rs:10–61`; matching limitations: S6]

6. **Loudness compensation is useful, but its fast estimate is not a measured LUFS result.** BS.1770 programme loudness uses time-dependent gating. A long-term weighted spectrum alone cannot reconstruct which blocks pass those gates after EQ. The inspected `EqLoudnessModel` correctly describes itself as an estimate. The main agent subsequently verified that its input is mean power per FFT bin in log-spaced ranges, normalized with the Hann sum (`analysis/spectrum.rs`, around line 150). The frequency factor is therefore appropriate for this relative-delta log quadrature; do not remove it. Approximate K weighting and absent temporal gating remain estimation limits. Keep compensation on by default, distinguish it from listening-only A/B matching, and show when full-track measurements replace estimates. [S1, Annex 1, equations (1)–(7); S8, “Spectrum/RTA controls,” Mode; local evidence: `eq_loudness.rs:12–38,41–71`; spectrum-producer verification supplied by main agent, not independently repeated here]

7. **Consistency means a shared revision and processing definition, followed by format-specific validation.** The README describes the right architecture: shared live/fixed processing, warmed states, background full-track preparation, export snapshots and stale-result protection. Retain QA at seeks, tails and pending revisions. Dither, sample-rate conversion and lossy encoding mean exported samples need not equal preview samples. Apple documents encoding-related peak changes and recommends auditioning encodes. Compare settled, aligned PCM paths for processor consistency, then decode each actual delivery format and report its own loudness and true peak. A nominal ceiling or short lookahead is not proof that every encoded result satisfies that ceiling. [S4, “Mastering Tools,” printed p. 6, and “Previewing the Converted Audio File,” p. 8; local evidence: `README.md:17,66–73`]

**Scientifically unsupported implications to prevent**

| Potential implication | Evidence-based correction and current status |
| --- | --- |
| “−14 LUFS is the mastering standard,” or “Streaming safe is safe on every service.” | Platform playback policy and delivery advice have specific scopes. Current help risks overgeneralization; change wording, not all targets. [S1–S4] |
| “The genre target or lower tonal error proves better/professional sound.” | No validation of the legacy curves was supplied or performed. README already calls genre/artifact scores heuristics; preserve that qualification everywhere. |
| “A detected spectral bump is a defect.” | The suggestion uses spectral shape, not causal separation of resonance, notes, arrangement or taste. Call it a proposed tone adjustment. |
| “The +1 dB bass/air cap bounds total boost,” or “seven small moves guarantee transparency.” | Caps constrain individual parameters. Combined response, headroom and guard activity still require verification. |
| “Hold loudness is exact throughout a drag,” or “a K-weighted spectrum is an EBU meter.” | The inspected model is approximate; gated full-track measurements and current-revision status remain essential. The main agent has verified the bandwidth assumption; remaining limitations concern approximation and gating, not a demonstrated frequency-factor bug. [S1; main-agent source verification] |
| “The spectrum should look flat,” or “matching the displayed curve matches the sound.” | Analyzer slope/bandwidth are representation choices. Reference matching also depends on programme content. [S6–S8] |
| “Sample peak = true peak,” “ceiling = LUFS,” or “fast Rust = equivalent/better sound.” | BS.1770 separates loudness and reconstructed true-peak measurement; a level target is not a peak limit. Performance timing supports only the measured speed claim. README already distinguishes revised DSP and listening acceptance. [S1, Annexes 1–2] |

**Primary sources, exact sections and applicability**

All links were checked during this research on November 12, 2026. Manufacturer material supports principles/workflows; it does not certify this implementation or its numerical heuristics.

- **S1 — ITU-R BS.1770-5 (11/2023), in force.** [Official recommendation/status](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-BS.1770/en); [English recommendation PDF](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/bs/R-REC-BS.1770-5-202311-I!!PDF-E.pdf). Annex 1, “Specification of the objective multichannel loudness measurement algorithm,” especially filtering and equations (1)–(7); Annex 2, “Guidelines for accurate measurement of ‘true-peak’ level.” The official index lists this as the in-force revision; this is measurement guidance, not a music target curve.
- **S2 — EBU R 128, version 5.0, November 2023.** [Recommendation PDF](https://tech.ebu.ch/docs/r/r128.pdf), printed pp. 3–4, recommendation items g–m: −23 LUFS, gating and programme true-peak policy in its broadcast scope.
- **S3 — Spotify for Artists, “Loudness normalization.”** [Official support page](https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/loudness-normalization/), “How we adjust loudness,” “How listeners adjust loudness,” and “Mastering tips.” Current playback settings, album context, exceptions and delivery advice; no claim about other services.
- **S4 — Apple, “Apple Digital Masters: Music as the Artist and Sound Engineer Intended.”** [Official PDF](https://www.apple.com/apple-music/apple-digital-masters/docs/apple-digital-masters.pdf), “Loudness, Dynamic Range, and Clipping,” printed pp. 4–5; “Loudness Normalization,” p. 5; “Mastering Tools,” p. 6; “Previewing the Converted Audio File,” p. 8. It supports Apple's stated approach, not an inferred numerical normalization target.
- **S5 — iZotope, Tonal Balance Control Help, “Meters and Target Curves.”** [Manufacturer manual](https://downloads.izotope.com/docs/tonal-balance-control/meters-and-target-curves/index.html), “Target Curves,” “Broad View,” “Fine View” and custom-target instructions. This is an older manual used as a documented design precedent, not a claim about the newest product's dataset or performance.
- **S6 — FabFilter Pro-Q 4 Help, “EQ Match.”** [Manufacturer manual](https://www.fabfilter.com/help/pro-q/using/eqmatch), “Step 1: Choose your reference,” “Step 2: Match,” and “Notes.” Supports time-averaged reference capture, adjustable matching detail and separate acceptance of proposed bands.
- **S7 — FabFilter Pro-Q 4 Help, “Spectrum analyzer.”** [Manufacturer manual](https://www.fabfilter.com/help/pro-q/using/analyzer), analyzer-panel options, especially Resolution, Speed and Tilt. A display convention is not an EQ target.
- **S8 — REW Help, “RTA Window.”** [Developer manual](https://www.roomeqwizard.com/help/help_en-GB/html/spectrum.html), “Spectrum/RTA controls,” Mode, FFT Length, Averaging and Window; “Adjust RTA Levels.” Used only for analyzer bandwidth/normalization distinctions, not room correction or mastering targets.
- **S9 — EBU Loudness Test Set, version 5.0, published March 30, 2016.** [Official test package and description](https://tech.ebu.ch/publications/ebu_loudness_test_set). The page links the 70-file package and EBU Tech 3341/3342; use applicable test descriptions and tolerances. The package's publication date is distinct from the 2023 R 128 revision.
